Size, detail and references

Detail needs space. Most microrealism pieces are between 5 and 15 cm. Faces and portraits need more room than a single animal or object, so that the details stay readable as the tattoo ages.

The reference matters as much as the needle: the sharper and better lit the photo, the more precise the result. For portraits, send several photos and Roman will tell you honestly which one works best.

Session and healing

Microrealism sessions often take three to six hours, and larger pieces sometimes need two sessions. You get breaks whenever you need them. Because of the design time, microrealism is booked as an appointment rather than a walk-in.

Soft shading heals best when it stays out of the sun, sea and pool for about two weeks. After healing, SPF 50 keeps the grey tones clear in the Cyprus sun.

Microrealism questions

What is the difference between microrealism and fine line?

Fine line is about delicate linework. Microrealism adds soft shading, light and depth, so the motif looks like a small photograph. Many designs combine both.

How do microrealism tattoos age?

With sensible size, proper aftercare and sun protection, microrealism tattoos age well. Very small pieces packed with detail soften faster, which is why size planning matters.

How long does a microrealism session take?

Usually three to six hours. Larger or very detailed pieces may be split into two sessions.

Can you tattoo a portrait from my photo?

Yes, portraits of people and animals are possible from a sharp, well-lit photo. If a reference is not detailed enough, Roman will tell you before the session.
